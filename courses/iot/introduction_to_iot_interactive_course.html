STEP 1: THE REVOLUTION

What Makes a "Thing" Smart?

A traditional appliance requires human hands to close a circuit. An IoT device introduces wireless transmission, a computing brain, and an actuator.

Isolated (Mechanical Circuit): Power is switched directly through manual contact. Without human presence, no action occurs.
Connected (IoT Pipeline): Radio signals send telemetry to an MCU (ESP32) which computes decisions and triggers a relay.
Key takeaway: IoT replaces manual switches with programmable logic and wireless triggers.

STEP 2: THE DEFINITION

The Core Definition

Coursera

"The Internet of Things, or IoT, is a network of physical devices that can transfer data to one another without human intervention."

— Coursera IoT Specialization
1. Physical Devices: Everyday objects embedded with sensors, compute, and radio chips.
2. Transfer Data: Continuous telemetry streaming via lightweight protocols (MQTT, BLE, LoRa).
3. Without Human Intervention: Machine-to-Machine (M2M) autonomy where systems act instantaneously on their own.
Takeaway: If a human has to push a button every time, it's just a remote control—not true IoT.

STEP 3: THE ENGINE

The Feedback Loop

Every autonomous IoT installation operates on a continuous 3-stage loop:

1. Sense (Sensor)
Reads analog environment (temperature, light, moisture) into electrical digits.
2. Decide (Compute)
Edge controller or Cloud rule engine checks: if (temp > 30°C).
3. Act (Actuator)
Motor, relay, valve, or LED turns digital decisions back into physical action.

STEP 5: CONNECTIVITY

How IoT Speaks

There is no "one protocol to rule them all". Engineers select radios based on the range vs. power vs. bandwidth triangle.

Golden Rule:

Long Range + High Bandwidth = Huge Battery Drain.

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)

Short Range • Ultra Low Power
Signal Range: ~10 to 30 meters
Data Bandwidth: Moderate (~1 - 2 Mbps)
Battery Efficiency: Months to Years on Coin Cell
Best Suited For:

Wearable fitness trackers, smart beacons, medical monitors, and peripheral sensor tags syncing with a nearby smartphone.

Triple I2C Bus Hub (4x3)

I2C Interface (4 Rows x 3 Columns)

3.3V Power & Logic

The board provides a 3-column I2C expansion port labeled "I2C" arranged in 4 rows: • Row 1 (Top): SDA (Serial Data Line -> micro:bit Pin 20) • Row 2: SCL (Serial Clock Line -> micro:bit Pin 19) • Row 3: GND (System Ground / 0V) • Row 4 (Bottom): 3V3 (3.3V DC Power Rail) All 3 columns are wired in parallel to the exact same I2C bus. Connect up to 3 I2C modules simultaneously without external breadboards.

Hardware Parameters ID: I2C_PORT
micro:bit Mapping SDA = P20 | SCL = P19
Functionality Daisy-chain up to 3 I2C sensors in parallel
Onboard Conflicts Shares bus with OLED (0x3C)
