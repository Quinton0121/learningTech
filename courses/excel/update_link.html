Before we write formulas, we need to understand the grid. It's simpler than you think.

STEP 4: THE SECRET KEY

The Magic Equals Sign

By default, if you type "5+5" into a cell, Excel just thinks it's a piece of text.

To tell Excel you want it to act as a calculator, you must start with an equals sign (=). This is called Syntax.

Try it out in the Formula Bar below:

  1. Type 10+20
  2. Then, add an equals sign at the front: =10+20

STEP 5: POINT AND CLICK

Building Formulas by Clicking

You don't have to type cell addresses manually. It's much faster to just click them! Let's try calculating =A2-B1.

Follow these steps:

  • 1. Select the cell C2
  • 2. Type the equal sign (=)
  • 3. Click cell A2 (320)
  • 4. Type the minus sign (-)
  • 5. Click cell B1 (39)
  • 6. Hit the Enter button

You did it! 281 is correct.

Press the Right Arrow to continue.

STEP 6: ENTIRE ROWS & COLUMNS

Selecting Everything

Need to select an entire line of data? You can click the Column Letter or the Row Number at the edges of the grid. Excel uses a colon without numbers to indicate a full column (like A:A).

Click on a Letter (A-D) or Number (1-4) on the grid:

STEP 7: RANGES

Grouping Cells Together

A Range is a collection of two or more cells. Instead of writing =A1+A2+A3+A4, Excel uses a colon (:) to connect the first and last cells.

Click and drag on the grid to create a range:

Excel's Calculator Keys

Excel can do any math you want, but the symbols might look a little different than a math textbook.
